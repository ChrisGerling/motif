The full story

What MOTIF and Cardinal are, and how every stage works — from the idea to the verified millimetres. Each chapter is linkable; jump from the navigation above.

01

The idea: author the scene, then measure

MOTIF starts from an uncomfortable fact about human motion recovery: for video from the internet, nobody knows where the person truly was. The field's ground truth lives in two places, and neither is the world we want to work in — motion-capture stages, where the trajectory is known but the scene is a lab, and pixel-space or scale-solved metrics, which quietly give up on metric placement. Text-to-video models add a third gap: they produce plausible motion, but no metric 3D trajectory and no editable scene to put it in.

The closed-loop-2D trap

The most seductive wrong answer is the overlay. Run HMR on a generated clip, reproject the result onto the video it came from, and it "looks identical" — as it must: 2D reprojection is correct by construction and says nothing about the global 3D trajectory. The poster's floating-body exhibit makes this concrete: a reconstruction can sit exactly on its pixels while the body floats above the floor — or, in a measured case, sinks 6–8 cm into it. The numbers tell the same story at corpus scale: PA-MPJPE 31 mm and MPJPE 56 mm coexist with a global position error of 110 mm — the global error is 3.5× the local one. Articulation is largely a solved problem; where the body is is not, and the overlay cannot see the difference.

The move: build the correct answer first

MOTIF inverts the usual setup. Instead of estimating camera and scene from footage, the scene is authored first, in metres: room and props built in Blender, camera intrinsics and extrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy, W, H, matrix_world), floor plane, scene meshes and expected contacts — all recorded in a GT contract (meta.json) and self-checked to 0.000 px reprojection and 0.00 mm placement. Bookend poses pin the start and end of the motion. The correct answer exists before reconstruction ever runs. Two things follow:

  • Every estimator is seeded, not guessing. Known camera, known geometry and bookend anchors are injected into each stage — camera injection, box injection, geometric floor contacts — turning the ill-posed in-the-wild problem into a known-scene one.
  • Generated video becomes a measurable motion source. A Veo clip has no ground-truth trajectory of its own, so its reconstruction is judged against the known scene — floor contact, penetration, whether the 3D path does what the clip shows. Absolute error is measured by taking the generator out of the loop entirely: render a known motion through the known camera, reconstruct it, and compare in millimetres.

Thesis. Author the scene, know the camera, seed every estimator — and gate every prior on evidence.

02

Cardinal: one agent that builds worlds

MOTIF is the science; Cardinal (working title) is the operator. It is not an external app poking at Blender — it is a Blender add-on whose agent loop runs on a background thread, with every bpy touch marshalled to the main thread. The user states a goal in plain language; Cardinal authors the stage, runs the capture, and edits the result.

A supervisor and three specialists

Brain — orchestratorClaude Opus (claude-opus-4-8 default) plans and drives the whole run
Painter — image generationgemini-3-pro-image: style boards, target-room renders, bookends
Director — video generationveo-3.1-generate-preview: turns bookend frames into the source clip
Eyes — visual verifiergemini-3.6-flash: an independent reviewer, not a subroutine

Every image a tool returns is also sent to the Eyes, which attaches a structured eyes_review verdict to the tool result. The Brain must read it before drawing any visual conclusion — a missing or errored review can never be reported as a pass.

111 tools, one loop

Cardinal registers 111 tools inside Blender, in four families: scene building (PolyHaven / AmbientCG / BlenderKit import, texturing, block-out, clutter), self-audit (geometry / function / navigation), scenario authoring (cameras, bookends, motion specs) and MOTIF orchestration (extract_motion, HMR track checks, apply_result_to_scene). Transports are provider-neutral: API key, claude.ai subscription, or a ChatGPT/Codex account.

Deliberately not an MCP server. Tools are plain decorated Python handed to the model as tools=. What that buys: control over the loop (pause, steer, abort, hard turn caps), persistent plans and numbered runs on disk, deterministic domain tools instead of freeform scripting, a closed verify loop, and traceable completion evidence. Each run is a numbered results_v2/runs/NNNN_slug/ directory with a full trace.jsonl; the run tracker streams it live and can steer mid-run — steer text rides into the agent's next tool result.

Style-first authoring

Rooms are designed before they are built. The order is fixed: a style board (set_style, once), a 2D floor plan on paper — zones, then items with center, size and rotation in metres, each with an explicit floor/surface/wall/ceiling placement contract — then a Painter target-room image rendered from the confirmed plan and pinned as the build target. Only then geometry, high to low: shell and structure, per-zone object placement, materials and generated decals (wallpaper, murals, bespoke tiles), the lighting rig with practicals, and finally clutter. Every dimension is a written-down number in the plan — and ultimately in the GT contract (meta.json: fx, fy, cx, cy, W, H, matrix_world, floor, meshes, contacts; reprojection self-check 0.000 px), not an estimate.

Design, verify, repair

Nothing is trusted by look. fix_orientation renders each directional piece from four sides plus top-down and rotates it to face its real target; verify_group renders each zone and returns problem/fix pairs plus hard-measured geometry_issues. finalize_scene is the release gate — architecture, ownership, navigation and a multi-angle QA pack judged by the independent Eyes — and any later scene mutation invalidates it, forcing a fresh pass.

Evidence of autonomy

  • 75 numbered runs from natural-language prompts; 116 agent-authored motion specs; 28 agent scenario runs.
  • The four demo scenes — Andy's Room, spaceship cockpit, Mediterranean kitchen, Scandi packing station — are themselves Cardinal builds.
  • Two overnight autonomous builds — a Gothic dark-academia library and a Japanese zen garden — ran unattended with ask_user disabled: every choice was the agent's, recorded in the final summary, and the finalize ledger, not the agent, decides when they count as done.
03

Generating the take: Veo between two authored frames

MOTIF never asks Veo for a shot from nothing. Every take is conditioned on two images rendered from the authored scene through the authored camera: the first frame and the last. Veo 3.1 accepts a last_frame condition alongside the start image (pipeline_v2/veo/veo_gen_bookend.py), and given both keyframes it interpolates between them. Duration is deliberately omitted — the API rejects duration_seconds together with last_frame (400 "use case not supported"); the interpolation sets its own length, in practice 8 s at 24 fps, with bookends rendered at 1280×720.

Why both endpoints

Asking politely for a locked camera does not work, and neither does trusting a score that says it did: our earlier camera_lock_score read 1.00 on every archived clip, including one whose background moved across 63% of the frame. Bookending removes the choice instead of requesting it — the first and last pixels of the take are already the authored camera's view of the authored geometry, so the camera has nowhere to go. The "no pan, no zoom" negatives move into the dedicated negative_prompt slot rather than being smeared through the main prompt, which stays positive. On the pp24 pilot batch the drift check reads 0.0003° at p75: locked.

The four-part prompt and the stand-ins

Each scenario spec compiles into four prompt blocks (build_existing_scene_scenario.py):

scenethe furnished room shown in both input images; preserve every wall, prop, material and light
stand-inthe same single adult in both bookends — explicit gender, identity, proportions and clothing locked; never morph, duplicate or remove
motionthe requested action, in plain language
camera + compositionfixed locked-off tripod, statics pinned to the same pixels, whole person visible, silent audio, plus the NEGATIVE list

The stand-ins in the two bookend renders are posed bodies from a named pose-template library (neutral, walk, low_bed_sit, …), each placed by two floor-plane anchors — pelvis_xy and face_xy at z = 0. Seated ends take their pelvis height from the support contract of the actual furniture, not from a universal offset.

The action gate: did the person do the thing?

Coherence checking is not adherence checking: a 2026-08-11 lens pair passed the coherence verifier clean while the man pivoted in place instead of walking his route (0.126 m recovered vs 0.608 m authored). So verify_action.py runs two independent judges. Eyes (Gemini 2.5 Flash) watches the full video three times — a single call flipped its answer on consecutive runs of the same clip, so a majority of three votes decides. Brain (Claude) judges an 8-frame filmstrip without seeing Eyes' answer. Verdicts are structured — pass/fail/inconclusive — and fail closed: a missing or unparseable verdict is inconclusive, never pass. One confident "no" from either judge fails the take. A model-free corroborator, net vs total travel of the subject's image centroid, is recorded alongside but never gates.

Money is only spent after a preflight: run_veo refuses to generate without a fresh verify_bookends verdict — a VLM plus a 3D-geometry gate on the two seed frames, costing cents before Veo costs dollars — and a stale or 3D-blocked preflight blocks the call.

A real rejection. Take motif_pp24_andy_turn_scan_v1 asked for "slowly turn on the spot, scanning the whole room from the bed around to the door". Veo delivered a man who walks from the bed across the room to the door. Eyes: 0/3 say performed, confidence 0.95 — "walked across the room instead of remaining on the spot". Brain, seeing only eight stills, was fooled into a soft yes; the full-video judge was not. The pre-HMR contract recorded release_to_hmr: false with blocker action:fail, and the take was resampled. The verdict binds the SHA-256 of the exact video bytes and instruction, so no later clip can ride on an old pass.

04

From pixels back to metres: seeded reconstruction and grounding

Everything downstream of the video is monocular 3D reconstruction — the regime where estimators fail worst. MOTIF's answer is simple: never let a model estimate what the authored scene already knows. The camera intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy) and extrinsics are exact by construction, and they are injected into both pose extractors rather than recovered from pixels: PromptHMR runs with the authored focal length forced, and DuoMo receives the full authored camera. Even person detection is seeded — on stylised renders DuoMo's own detector produced 0/192 valid boxes, so boxes are injected from the PromptHMR track instead: 192/192.

Withhold the focal and watch it break

Give PromptHMR its default 1280 px focal instead of the authored 497.8 px — everything else identical — and the error jumps by +70 mm GPE (110 → 180 mm, 7/8 sequences worsen, p = 0.016) and +48 mm MPJPE (56 → 104 mm, 8/8, p = 0.008). Camera seeding is not a convenience; it is a measured, double-digit-millimetre prior.

The lift: one matrix, no fitting

Extractor output lives in the camera frame. It reaches the scene in a single closed-form step — p = M · diag(1,−1,−1) · p_cam: the CV-to-Blender axis flip, then the camera's known matrix_world. No alignment, no Umeyama, no fitting to ground truth. On the real-capture scenes this raw lift is already competitive (DuoMo: 87 mm scene-frame GPE vs 101 mm for the full PromptHMR arm), but on the procedural corpus it passes depth error straight through (220 vs 110 mm) — which is precisely what the grounding stages exist to absorb.

Grounding: three operations, no typed coordinates

  • Metric scale — one scale from the weighted agreement of authored evidence (body height, floor contact, object depth), applied along camera rays via fx_true / f_hmr, so depth becomes metric while the 2D overlay does not move a pixel.
  • Two-point anchor warp — the trajectory is pinned to the authored bookend poses at both ends: translation only, articulation untouched, anchors landing within 5 mm.
  • Contact and penetration corrections — anti-skate on planted feet, a floor clamp against the known plane, and a ballistic gate that refuses to treat a leg swing as flight without ≥ 20 mm of measured pelvis rise (dance 86 → 64 mm, crouch 126 → 113 mm).

Every operation reads geometric evidence out of the GT contract. Nobody types a coordinate, and no language model ever nudges a number.

The prior we had to turn off

One stage did not survive contact with real motion. The retimer — a linear-path-progress prior that overwrites the extractor's measured timing — flattered the procedural corpus, whose gaits are constant-speed by construction. On real stand-then-walk captures it was catastrophic: 595 and 1039 mm GPE with it, 137 and 129 mm without. Every headline number therefore runs one recipe with --retime 0. The lesson generalises, and the next chapter is built on it: every post-processing stage is a prior, and a prior deserves trust only against evidence.

05

Verification: gates, metrics and honest caveats

Everything in MOTIF rests on two commitments: measure with the literature's own definitions, and never let a prior win without evidence. This chapter states the measurement machinery — and its limits — in full.

The GT contract

Every authored scene carries a machine-checked ground-truth contract in meta.json: camera intrinsics (fx, fy, cx, cy, W, H), camera extrinsics (per-frame if animated), the floor plane, every prop and scene mesh with its world transform, and the expected contacts. Mesh sidecars are hash-bound — sha256 on the file plus separate geometry_sha256, topology_sha256 and collision_sha256 per mesh (pipeline_v2/harness/gt_schema.json) — so a metric can never silently run against stale geometry. The contract self-checks at 0.000 px reprojection and 0.00 mm placement.

The GT-free impact gate

Post-processing is a prior, and priors can hurt: on one scene, anchoring degraded a 41 mm prior to 98 mm. The impact gate (pipeline_v2/cleanup/impact_gate.py) therefore decides per clip, without ground truth, whether grounding improved on the known-camera prior: it reprojects both through the authored camera and scores them against independent ViTPose keypoints — the one measurement the grounding does not fit to — with a floor-validity guardrail so a pixel-perfect but floating prior cannot win. Measured: 4/4 correct choices, keeping the grounding where the prior sinks 6–8 cm into the floor and returning the prior where grounding moved the body off the pixels. Gated arm: 50 [43, 56] mm GPE.

The metric set, one breath each

GPE
Joint error in the world frame, no alignment at all — the number MOTIF exists to fix.
MPJPE
Mid-hip-relative joint error per frame — articulation plus body orientation, translation removed.
PA-MPJPE
Per-frame similarity (Procrustes) alignment — pure articulation, everything global forgiven.
RTE
Root error after one whole-sequence rigid Umeyama, ÷ path length, in % — the only world metric that does not solve scale, but a single rigid fit absorbs linear drift, so it is never reported alone.
W/WA-MPJPE100
Error per 100-frame segment, similarity-aligned on the first two frames (W) or all frames (WA).
Jitter
Motion smoothness in 10 m/s³, needs no GT — and is never quoted alone, because a perfectly smooth trajectory can be perfectly wrong.

Headline numbers

  • 41 mm GPE on the best real-capture clip (TRUMANS walk).
  • Corpus (n=8) error cascade: PA-MPJPE 31 → MPJPE 56 → GPE 110 [87, 139] mm — global placement is 3.5× local articulation.
  • RTE 1.4%; published EMDB-2 context values: GVHMR 1.9, WHAM 4.1 (different dataset — context, not comparison).
  • Raw PromptHMR world output: W-MPJPE₁₀₀ 769 mm vs ours 102 mm (8/8 sequences, p=0.008).
  • Real-capture arm progression (GPE, 4 TRUMANS scenes): 101 → 87 → 60 → 50 mm.

Honest caveats

  • Rendered ground truth. A known motion rendered through a known camera measures reconstruction under known geometry — it says nothing about real footage. Precedent: AGORA, BEDLAM, SURREAL; the domain gap stands.
  • Ablations, not baselines. Every ablation arm still receives the bookend anchors the reconstruction structurally requires; the zero-GT baseline (CRISP's own pipeline) is not yet run.
  • The focal ablation is a lower bound. It varies one number, not "the camera": extrinsics, principal point and grounding are identical in both arms.
  • Context numbers are not comparisons. EMDB-2 values are quoted only to make magnitudes readable.
  • Aggregation is per sequence, then across sequences — never pooled over frames.
  • CI coverage at small n. The 95% BCa intervals measure ~78% coverage at n=6 on heavy-tailed error, missing mostly at the upper end — read them as optimistic-width.
06

Beyond the trajectory: hands, reskin, cleanup, the viewer

The trajectory is the thesis; around it sits a ring of smaller stages that make the delivered take usable. Each one follows the same house rule: it is a prior, and it only gets to act where evidence says it helps.

Hand articulation graft

pipeline_v2/cleanup/hand_wilor.py grafts WiLoR's 15-joint MANO finger articulation onto the reconstruction; the wrist stays owned by the body track, so the graft can refine fingers but never move the arm. The gate is per side, and it targets a specific failure: at small hand sizes WiLoR misclassifies left and right, and the signature of that failure is sporadic single-frame detections. The measured separator is clean — 0–1% isolated detections on sides where the graft improves things, 14–27% on sides where it degrades them (pose jitter did not separate the two). Gated result on covered sides: hand-local joint error 115.8→108.6 mm and 19.7→17.4 mm; every rejected side is left byte-identical, so the net effect is strictly non-negative. Honest caveat: the thresholds come from four measured sides; corpus-scale validation is pending.

Reskin: the take is not married to SMPL-X

To show the output is a motion asset rather than a model-specific artifact, a delivered SMPL-X take was retargeted onto a Mixamo toon rig — a completely different character, same trajectory through the same authored room. Nothing in the pipeline is re-run; the retarget consumes the finished take. If the metric path is right, it is right on any rig you skin over it.

Evidence-gated despike

HMR occasionally emits a single-burst glitch — an acceleration outlier lasting up to a few frames. The despike stage replaces such bursts with linear interpolation between the nearest clean neighbour frames, but only if two checks pass: mean reprojection against independent ViTPose 2D keypoints must not worsen beyond a 1.5 px deadband (the same deadband the impact gate uses), and a floor guard must hold. Bursts longer than the cap are reported, not fixed — rewriting motion is not despiking. Jerk drops; articulation on clean frames is untouched.

The graft and the despike are the same idea twice: 2D pixel evidence through the known camera is the one measurement the reconstruction never fitted to, so it can arbitrate what the reconstruction is allowed to change.

The in-browser viewer

The deliverable is inspectable without Blender. Rooms are exported as glTF under poly and texture budgets; each motion take ships as a quantized uint16 vertex stream — roughly 8–12 MB gzipped per take — decoded in the browser and played back in three.js with orbit controls.

  • No re-derivation: the streams are built from the same npz data the comparison videos render, so what you orbit is what was measured.
  • Free camera: the closed-loop-2D trap dissolves when the viewer can leave the authored viewpoint and look at the path from above.
07

Limitations and where this goes

Every number in this work was measured under conditions we can state precisely — and those conditions are the limitations. We would rather enumerate them than have a reviewer do it.

Rendered ground truth, stated as such

Absolute trajectory error requires ground truth, and ours comes from known motion rendered through a known camera. The benchmark can claim that reconstruction under a known camera and known geometry reaches a stated error, and that withholding a specific prior changes it by a stated amount. It cannot claim anything about real camera footage. Rendered-data benchmarks are established practice — AGORA (CVPR 2021), BEDLAM (CVPR 2023), SURREAL — but the domain gap belongs next to the number. The Veo clips themselves have no ground truth at all: there we have only geometry consistency against the authored scene, independent 2D evidence, and the gates.

Anchors are load-bearing

Every ablation arm still receives the bookend anchors that reconstruct_from_bookends structurally requires — these are ablations, not baselines. Anchoring absorbs monocular depth error (the raw known-camera DuoMo lift reads 220 mm on the corpus where the full pipeline reaches 110), so the world metrics measure residual path shape under anchors, not free monocular reconstruction. The anchors' authority is itself a prior: on one scene it dragged a 41 mm estimate to 98 — exactly the failure the impact gate now catches (4/4 correct, GT-free). The zero-GT baseline, CRISP's own pipeline via pipeline_v2/eval/crisp_adapter.py, is still to be run. Gate thresholds come from small n; corpus-scale validation is pending.

Scope

  • Simple locomotion. Walks, crouches, turns, reaches. OOD motion is a stretch fixture, not a current target; TRUMANS windows are locomotion-dominant, and missing obj_locs leave some object contacts unverifiable.
  • Veo variance. Generated takes vary; the dual-judge gate (Gemini eyes, three votes; Claude brain) rejects bad ones, and a rejection costs a full regeneration — correctness bought with resampling compute.
  • Single-person scenes. One SMPL-X actor per capture; multi-person association is untouched.

Where this goes

  • A physics backbone. CRISP-style (arXiv 2512.14696) RL contact cleanup against our known clean geometry — the setting where a physics stage can absorb residual camera error.
  • Larger motion vocabularies beyond locomotion, under the same evidence-gated recipe.
  • Multi-take selection: generate several takes and let the gates choose, turning Veo variance from a cost into a search space.
  • Tighter autonomy: overnight scene-to-motion chains already run end-to-end — 75 numbered agent runs from natural-language prompts, 116 agent-authored motion specs, 28 agent scenario runs; all four showcase scenes are Cardinal builds.

The claim stays narrow on purpose: author the scene, know the camera, seed every estimator — and gate every prior on evidence. The roadmap adds better priors and stronger evidence, never looser gates.
